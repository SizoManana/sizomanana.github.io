What I am actually good at

Connecting evidence to an interface decision

Problem framing

Turning a broad client concern into a smaller set of user, product and commercial questions that can actually be investigated.

Structure before styling

Information architecture, user flow and low-fidelity wireframes before visual polish, so the difficult decisions are not hidden inside a beautiful screen.

Product conditions

Thinking through loading, empty, error, permissions, recovery and other states rather than treating the happy path as the whole product.

Measurement discipline

Defining the event model and baseline early enough that the post-launch result can be interpreted rather than reconstructed after the fact.

The role label can change.The common thread is that I am most useful when I can understand a messy problem, structure an experience, and stay close enough to the data to learn whether the decision held up.

Research in the work

The portfolio should show the evidence, not only say “research” in a skills list

My research practice is strongest where behavioural, commercial and evaluative evidence meet. I use the method that matches the question rather than treating research as one ceremony.

AnalyticsFunnels, event behaviour, segmentation, conversion movement and traffic context.
BehaviourSession recordings and interaction patterns used to contextualise quantitative signals.
EvaluationHeuristic, accessibility, content, message and information-architecture review.
ContextSecondary research into the market, business model, audience and competitive environment.

The Dayshape case is a good example of why this matters. A blended form-completion number suggested one problem; segmentation to the actual target customer exposed a different one. The value was not the chart. It was changing the question the team was trying to answer.

Where I am growing

Depth matters more to me than pretending the gaps are already closed

Primary research programmes

I have stronger experience in behavioural, secondary and evaluative research than in owning a large interview or survey programme end to end. That is a capability I want to deepen, not a gap I want to disguise.

Experimental statistics

I have worked with A/B testing platforms and significance outputs, but I want deeper hands-on rigour around power, sample-size planning and causal inference rather than relying on the platform layer.

Mentoring

I have often been the only person in my discipline, so much of my leadership has shown up through frameworks, templates and process rather than direct line management. I want the next step to include more people development.

AI-assisted practice
