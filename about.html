How I got here

2018 to 2020, finance administration

Accounting and financial admin at Arcfyre International. Not a design job, and for a while I assumed it was a detour. It turned out to be the reason I am comfortable sitting in front of a CFO and talking about a funnel in revenue terms rather than engagement terms. Most designers have to learn that language. I started in it.

2020 to 2022, co-founder

I co-founded Apollo Vinyls with a partner, a two person venture selling apparel, vinyl and digital design services, with a design academy attached that never quite worked. I built and ran the website, including the e-commerce and the lead capture.

Optimising a conversion path when it is your own money teaches you very quickly which parts of a page matter and which ones you were only fond of. We traded for 25 months and then stopped. That is also an education.

2022 to 2025, performance agency

Landing pages and creative at SearchKings Africa, a Google Ads and lead generation agency. Every page existed to convert paid traffic, which is a brutal and useful way to learn: you find out fast whether a design works, because somebody is paying for every visitor who bounces off it.

This is also where I started pushing user flows and information architecture into a team process that had been working straight from visual comps.

2025 to 2026, consulting and product

CRO and UX consulting at Evara, formerly Inbound FinTech, an Elite HubSpot partner for financial services. This is where the measurement discipline came from, and where the work widened out from marketing pages into product surfaces inside the CRM itself.

My role ends in September 2026 as part of a company restructure, which is why you are reading this.

What I actually believe about this work

If you cannot measure it, you do not get to claim it. Most of what gets called CRO is redecoration with a confident voice.

Measurement comes before design, not after

The most common thing I inherit is an account where the tracking is wrong and nobody knows. Form starts firing on page view. Goals counting the wrong thing. A dashboard everybody trusts that is quietly describing a different website. Redesigning on top of that is not optimisation, it is guessing with extra steps.

So on a new account I audit and rebuild the instrumentation, then collect sixty to ninety days of clean data before I write a single hypothesis. Clients find that slow. It is the only way the recommendations end up being about their users instead of about my taste.

Constraints produce better decisions than freedom does

The best design work I have done was inside a platform that would not let me write a line of CSS. Having no room to invent my way out of a problem meant every choice had to be justified against something real, in that case the actual pixel width of the components available. I did not enjoy it at the time. The output was better than it would have been with a blank canvas.

Failure states are the design

Most design work ships the happy path and leaves engineering to invent the error states. That is backwards. The error states are where a user decides whether to trust a tool, and a tool nobody trusts does not get used regardless of how good the main flow looks.

Surfacing an open question beats deciding it quietly

When I do not know something that affects the build, I annotate it and hand it over as an open question rather than picking an answer and hoping. Guesses buried in a design file are the expensive kind, because nobody finds out they were guesses until the build is half finished.

A number you cannot defend is worse than no number

I would rather present one result I can walk through end to end, including what else changed at the same time and which part of the improvement was measurement correction rather than behaviour change, than three impressive figures I would stumble on under questioning. Being caught inflating is not recoverable.

Where I am honest about the gaps

I would rather you heard these from me than found them in an interview.

  • Statistical rigour. I have run A/B tests in VWO and Convert and read the significance the platforms report, but I have not run my own power calculations. On the traffic volumes I usually work with, most tests would be underpowered anyway, which is part of why I lean on properly instrumented before and after measurement over sixty to ninety days rather than pretending a two week test on thin traffic told us something. I am closing this gap deliberately.
  • Primary user research. I have not run interview or survey programmes. I work from behavioural data, session recordings and secondary research into the vertical and business model, triangulated against heuristic findings. That is real work but it is secondary research and I call it that. It is the capability I most want to add next.
  • Mentoring. I have been the only person in my discipline on the team, so I have not managed or mentored anyone directly. What I have done instead is build the processes, guidelines and templates the function runs on, and those were picked up by squads beyond my own. I would like a role where the next step is people rather than only process.
