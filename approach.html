Approach

Research, design and optimisation are one loop.

I use different methods depending on the problem, but the operating principle stays the same: understand the commercial and user context, gather evidence, form a hypothesis, design the intervention, make it measurable, then validate what changed.

01 · Understand

Start with the business problem, not the requested screen

The client request is rarely the problem statement. “The demo page is not converting” is an outcome. Before proposing a design change I want to know who the intended customer is, what action matters, where the journey starts, what the commercial value of that action is, and which constraints are real.

Commercial context

Business model, target audience, conversion path, priority services, traffic source and what a successful outcome is worth.

Experience context

Current journey, page purpose, information architecture, interaction constraints, existing platform behaviour and known implementation dependencies.

02 · Research

Use evidence to reduce the number of plausible explanations

A weak conversion rate can have several causes at once: low-intent traffic, unclear positioning, friction in the form, weak trust, mobile usability, poor sequencing or tracking that is simply wrong. Research helps decide which explanations deserve attention.

Behavioural and quantitative

GA4 funnels and events, segmentation, conversion movement, entry and exit behaviour, scroll and interaction data, and session recordings where available.

Evaluative and secondary

Heuristic review, competitive and vertical research, accessibility checks, content and message analysis, information-architecture review and relevant external research.

Research is not a decorative phase.The useful output is a decision: what we now believe is happening, what remains uncertain, and what evidence would change the recommendation.

03 · Hypothesise

One business problem can create several testable hypotheses

I do not treat a client problem as one giant hypothesis. A single outcome can have multiple contributing causes. The job is to separate them, prioritise them and define what evidence would support or weaken each one.

Problem: Demo conversion is below target.

Possible hypotheses: users do not understand the proposition quickly enough; trust is insufficient before the ask; the form creates too much friction; the CTA appears at the wrong point in the journey; or the traffic is not the right audience.

I prioritise opportunities by likely impact, effort and dependency. Some are quick interface or messaging changes. Others require content, development, tracking or a structural change to the journey.

04 · Design

Design the system, including the conditions where it does not behave perfectly

Structure comes before polish. I work through hierarchy, flow, information architecture and low-fidelity states before the final visual system. In product work, the happy path is only one state of the experience.

Core flow

  • Information architecture
  • User and task flow
  • Low-fidelity wireframes
  • Interaction and component decisions
  • Responsive behaviour

States and edge cases

  • Empty and loading states
  • Validation and error states
  • Partial or failed data
  • Permissions and unavailable actions
  • Recovery, retry and completion behaviour

Accessibility is considered across visual contrast and type, keyboard and focus behaviour, touch targets, content clarity, labels, semantics and media alternatives rather than as a final colour check.

05 · Instrument

A change is not ready for CRO validation if the event model cannot describe it

For optimisation work I define the tracking requirement alongside the design. The event name, trigger condition, relevant parameters, component location and related hypothesis need to be explicit before launch.

CTAClick intent, button text, location and page context.
FormsStart, submit, success and relevant multi-step progress.
EngagementTabs, accordions, downloads, video and other meaningful interactions.
QAGTM Preview, GA4 DebugView, parameter validation and duplicate-event checks.

Tracking is there to answer the hypothesis, not to collect events for their own sake.

06 · Validate

Separate movement from proof

Post-launch validation compares the baseline with the updated experience, then uses behavioural evidence to explain what may be driving the movement. The interpretation matters as much as the percentage change.

Correlation

The redesign launched and conversion increased. That is useful evidence, but other factors may have changed at the same time.

Causation

A controlled experiment isolates the experience change more effectively, giving stronger grounds to attribute the difference to the intervention.

Where traffic or implementation conditions do not support a valid controlled experiment, I describe before-and-after results as directional and document traffic, timing and known confounders instead of claiming the design caused the full uplift.
