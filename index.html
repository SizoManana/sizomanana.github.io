How I work

Most conversion work stops at the recommendation

Recommending a change is the easy half. Proving it did something means the measurement has to exist before you touch anything, and somebody has to come back three months later and check. That second part is where most CRO quietly falls over, and it is the part I own.

  1. Heuristic audit and scoringFront end and back end conversion factors, scored against a framework rather than judged by feel.
  2. Baseline captureMeasure before. Without this the after means nothing and you are just telling a story.
  3. InstrumentationGA4 and Google Tag Manager rebuilt where the default setup cannot answer the question. Custom events, dataLayer, real triggers, tested.
  4. HypothesisIf we change X then metric Y moves, because of mechanism Z. Ranked by impact against effort, not by preference.
  5. Design and buildWireframes and high fidelity in Figma, handed over in a state a developer can actually build from.
  6. Test where the volume supports itA/B testing in VWO, Convert, GA4 experiments or HubSpot. Where traffic is too thin for a valid test, I say so rather than pretending.
  7. Sixty to ninety days of dataLong enough to mean something.
  8. Validation and reportingAgainst the baseline and against the metrics set at the start, in Looker Studio and in a narrative leadership can act on.

On numbers

I will walk you through any figure on this site, including what else changed at the same time and which part of an improvement was measurement correction rather than behaviour change. A number I cannot defend is worth less to me than no number at all.

About this page

Every choice here was a conversion decision

It would be a strange thing to claim conversion expertise on a page built without any. So here is the reasoning, in case that is more useful to you than another paragraph about me.

The decisions, and why
  • One primary action, repeated. Get in touch sits in the header, the hero and the footer. Everything else is visually demoted so it does not compete for the same click.
  • Trust signals above the fold. Named clients, published work and the team award appear before any claim about me. Evidence first, assertion second, because someone who does not believe you stops reading.
  • Third party proof over self published proof. Where my employer has written up the work, I link to their page rather than only reproducing it here. Their domain is more credible than mine.
  • Videos do not load until you ask. An embedded player pulls real weight and sets cookies on arrival. Mine are a button until clicked, which keeps the page fast and does not track anyone who is not interested.
  • No web fonts. The system font stack removes a render blocking request and the flash of unstyled text, at the cost of some visual distinctiveness. A deliberate trade.
  • Accessible by construction. Semantic landmarks, a skip link, visible focus states, logical heading order, text contrast meeting WCAG 2.1 AA, reduced motion respected.
  • No layout shift. Image and video areas reserve their space before loading, so nothing jumps under your cursor.
  • Case studies lead with the constraint. Not the visuals. Reasoning is the thing a picture cannot show you.
