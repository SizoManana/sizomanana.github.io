CRO roadmap and site rebuild

A restrictive site holding a good product back

Dayshape makes AI powered resource management software used by professional services firms in 55 countries. The product was strong. The site was on a WordPress build that limited content flexibility, had an unclear value proposition and no conversion optimisation to speak of.

My roleCRO roadmap, site architecture and priority page design
ScopeWordPress to HubSpot migration, navigation, demo request and resources pages
RoadmapTwelve months, presented directly to their marketing lead
PublishedCase study on evara.co

The roadmap was the hard part, not the pages

A twelve month CRO roadmap means most of the value arrives late, and that is a difficult thing to sell to a marketing lead under quarterly pressure. So the sequencing has to earn its own case: what gets measured first, what gets fixed first, and why those are not the same list.

Month one was a resources page implementation. Month two was foundation setup, a full CRO audit and the hypothesis and implementation plan. The audit does not come first because you cannot audit what you cannot measure, and the measurement had to be built before anything it produced could be trusted.

The outcome I did not expect

They came back and bought a larger package. Not because the roadmap was persuasive as a document, but because seeing twelve months laid out made the cost of waiting visible. Sequencing honestly, including saying what would not be fixed until month eight, turned out to be a better commercial argument than promising everything at once.
