The actual problem was not the quoting

It was that six pillars of consulting services, each with sub-services and configurable parameters, do not fit in a person's head. A rep scoping a project was holding too much at once, so they guessed, and the guesses showed up later as margin erosion and scope arguments.

So the design work was mostly information hierarchy rather than interface decoration. What has to be visible at once, what can be progressively disclosed, and where a running total needs to sit so somebody notices before they commit rather than after.

What was designed

  • The end to end interface, from service selection through to a client facing quotation
  • A six pillar service catalogue structured into a usable hierarchy
  • Configuration controls for quantities, requirements and service specific parameters
  • How dynamic and module level pricing presents as the configuration changes
  • The review and confirmation experience for generated line items
  • The multi step quotation workflow, including discounts, delivery timelines, payment terms and final review
  • Interface states for editable values and the transfer of line items into the relevant HubSpot deal

On the two day turnaround

I am not holding this up as a virtue. Two days for an internal platform is fast because it had to be, not because rushing produces better work. What it did prove is that if you understand the operational problem well enough, the interface decisions come quickly. Most of the time lost in projects like this is spent working out what the business actually does.

What I would do differently

I would have insisted on sitting with two reps building a real quote before I designed anything. I worked from the process as described by RevOps, which was accurate, but described process and lived process are never quite the same thing and the gap is usually where the friction hides.
