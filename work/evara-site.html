Brand, IA and design system

Rebranding the consultancy I worked for, from structure to live system

Inbound FinTech became Evara. Design House created the brand style guide. My responsibility began where that guide met the product: information architecture, low fidelity structure, high fidelity UI in light and dark, reusable system behaviour and implementation through to the live site.

My roleSite design lead: IA, wireframes, high fidelity, interaction/system design and implementation direction
Brand partnerDesign House produced the visual identity and brand style guide
EvidenceBehaviour and content analysis from the previous Inbound FinTech site
ScopeHomepage, mega menu, search and filtering, page templates, landing pages, newsletter, light and dark
StatusDesigned, implemented and live
ProblemA new brand needed a site structure capable of explaining several consultancy offers without becoming a catalogue.
EvidencePrevious-site behaviour, content hierarchy and service architecture informed what needed to move, combine or become reusable.
Design moveResolve structure in low fidelity, then build paired light/dark UI and system components around that settled hierarchy.
OutcomeA live site system extending beyond the homepage into navigation, templates, campaigns and email.

Structure before styling

Low fidelity first, and defended

The structure was resolved in wireframes before brand decisions were applied. That kept stakeholder discussion on hierarchy, sequence, navigation and page purpose rather than colour or polish.

Paired interface states

The full page, both modes

Reusable behaviour

The system, not just the pages

A site handed over as a collection of finished pages decays when somebody needs a page the designer never drew. Search, filtering, navigation and reusable templates were therefore treated as system behaviour rather than decoration.

Product-design evidenceThe filter, search and navigation work includes states, variants and growth conditions. The design question was what happens as content expands, not only what the launch screen looks like.

Beyond the homepage

Landing pages and email

The rebrand was not finished when the main site launched. Campaign pages and email had to carry the same hierarchy and visual logic while operating under very different technical constraints.

Reflection

What I would change in the process

I would establish and name the component inventory before the high-fidelity stage rather than assembling it out of finished pages. The system pieces worked, but creating the inventory earlier would have reduced inconsistent naming and made developer handoff more explicit.

The useful lesson: a design system is easier to maintain when the component model exists before the pages, not when the component model is reverse engineered from them afterwards.
