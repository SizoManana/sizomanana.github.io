Redslim · UX research and CRO strategy

Turning buyer behaviour into a redesign roadmap

For a business that harmonises retail and consumer data, a website visit can involve a long evaluation rather than an immediate enquiry. My task was to understand those different journeys and give the redesign a research-led direction.

I developed behavioural research and segmentation around Redslim's existing marketing personas, then translated the findings into prioritised hypotheses and an implementation plan. That research informed a redesign delivered by another agency.

The question behind the redesign

Visitors could be researching the category, comparing solutions, assessing an enterprise supplier or returning to continue an earlier evaluation. Treating them all as ready to fill in a contact form would miss those differences.

I investigated where the website helped people move forward, where journeys stalled, and what information or interaction might support the next decision. The aim was to give the redesign team specific priorities rather than a list of generic conversion practices.

Building on personas with behavioural evidence

The marketing personas were an existing input. My contribution was developing the behavioural research and segmentation around them, connecting buyer needs with the paths people took through the site.

I combined five GA4 path explorations and two macro funnels with a qualitative review of session recordings. The analytics covered 26 July to 23 October 2025; the recording review drew from 14 to 23 October 2025.

The five segments were research-oriented visitors, solution seekers, enterprise buyers, returning visitors, and referral or partner visitors. These were working segments for planning, not verified identities assigned to every visitor.

Recordings helped identify pauses, repeat visits and interaction difficulties. They could suggest a need for clearer guidance or reassurance, but could not tell me a visitor's motivation with certainty. A returning browser also did not automatically mean an existing customer.

How the research changed the recommendations

I mapped the findings to the decision each visitor was trying to make. The examples below summarise that reasoning. They are recommendations, not claims of tested improvements.

Research before contact

Signal: Journeys moved through case studies and solution content without consistently progressing to an enquiry.

Recommendation: Put relevant next steps within the content, connecting evidence to a solution or an enquiry rather than relying on navigation alone.

Validation: Track onward progression and completed enquiries, not just CTA clicks.

Reassurance at the decision point

Signal: The research identified contact-stage friction and repeated evaluation of company and solution information.

Recommendation: Bring relevant proof and clear response expectations closer to the contact action, alongside simpler form interactions.

Validation: Measure form starts, completion and lead quality after confirming the tracking is reliable.

Different routes for different needs

Interpretation: Research-oriented visitors and enterprise buyers need different depth and types of evidence.

Recommendation: Structure solution, case-study and resource templates around the needs of each segment, with a relevant next step.

Validation: Compare meaningful journey progression by segment rather than relying only on the blended rate.

Make mobile actions easier to find

Signal: The review highlighted long-page navigation and interaction friction on mobile.

Recommendation: Test clearer CTA placement, shorter input flows and appropriate persistent actions.

Validation: Check usability and completed enquiries by device. Visibility alone is not conversion success.

A roadmap the next team could use

I produced a hypothesis document and a detailed implementation and segmentation plan. The 23 hypotheses were prioritised as high, medium or low; the implementation plan organised 20 workstreams into three stages.

01 · FOUNDATION

Clarify the route to enquiry

CTA strategy, trust signals, forms, engagement prompts and the contact experience.

02 · CONTENT AND JOURNEYS

Support the evaluation

Solution and case-study templates, resources, search, proof and segment-relevant content.

03 · REFINEMENT

Develop the supporting experience

Guided value estimation, long-page navigation, form patterns and company content.

The handoff connected audience needs, observed friction, proposed changes and measurement requirements. It gave the redesign team a basis for deciding what to tackle first and what still required validation.

What this project demonstrates

The outcome of my work was a documented research and CRO strategy that informed the website redesign. My ownership was the investigation, synthesis, segmentation, hypotheses and roadmap. Another agency owned the redesign's visual execution and build.

The 23 hypotheses were a proposed testing backlog, not 23 completed A/B tests. I do not have a verified post-launch conversion result attributable to this research, so the outcome I can demonstrate is the research, decision framework and handoff, rather than a percentage uplift.

What I would strengthen: reconcile funnel definitions and denominators, then validate the most important behavioural interpretations with direct user feedback and properly instrumented experiments.
