CRO and full site redesign

Rebuilding a compliance platform site around intent

Ruleguard is a regtech platform for compliance teams in regulated firms. The old site led with an abstract promise and a wall of award badges. Sites need refreshing every three to four years anyway, but this one had a specific problem: nothing above the fold showed you what the product actually did.

My roleCRO and design lead: audit, analysis, hypotheses, design, instrumentation
ScopeFull site. Home, platforms, workflows, sectors, solutions, pillars, functions, plus four child page templates and mobile
InputsData analysis, heuristic audit, hypothesis documents, session recordings
DeliverablesDesign system application, a sitewide tag manager trigger schema, two strategy documents
StatusDesigned and delivered, currently live in build

What changed and why

  • The hero shows the product working. Active feeds, hub counts, regional rule counts. For a compliance buyer the question is not whether you have a philosophy, it is whether your coverage includes their jurisdictions.
  • Two calls to action instead of one, at different commitment levels. Book a demo for the ready, see how it works for the researching, because a single high commitment button loses everyone who is not there yet.
  • Objection handling at the point of decision. No sales pitch, 30 minute walkthrough, ISO 27001 and 42001. The three things that stop a compliance officer clicking, answered in the same eye movement as the button.
  • Award badges moved down. They are credibility, not a value proposition, and they were occupying the most valuable space on the site.

The document where I disagreed with the client

The client had valid concerns about parts of the strategy. Rather than either capitulating or arguing, I wrote the review in a fixed three part structure, repeated for each area of disagreement: the client's valid concern, then the strategic counter point, then the recommendation.

Naming their concern as valid first is not politeness. It is the thing that makes the counter point readable, because nobody evaluates an argument fairly while they still think they have not been heard.

Phase one delivery, page by page

The user journey work was split into a webinar fix and a platform fix, each stated as a problem, a diagnosis and a set of specific changes rather than a general direction.

The rest of the system

A site this size is templates, not pages. Seven parent page types, four child page templates, and a mobile design for each, so the pattern holds when the client's marketing team adds a sector page I never saw.

The unglamorous deliverable I am most confident about

A sitewide Google Tag Manager trigger schema. Every event, every parameter, every trigger, written down before the build rather than reverse engineered afterwards. It is the least interesting document I produced on this account and the only one that determines whether any of the rest can be measured.

What I would do differently

I would have shipped the FAQ and webinar changes on their own first. They were the highest intent, lowest risk pages on the site, and releasing them ahead of the full redesign would have produced a clean read on a small change instead of one large release I can only measure in aggregate. I made the same mistake on BSO, which is how I know it is a habit rather than a one off.
