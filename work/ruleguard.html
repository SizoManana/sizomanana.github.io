Selling to people who are professionally paid to be sceptical

Ruleguard is a UK RegTech platform. Compliance software has a conversion problem that most B2B sites do not: the buyer's job is to find the flaw in what you are telling them. The site has to answer doubt before it asks for anything.

The FAQ page is not a support page

On most sites the FAQ is where you put things you could not fit elsewhere. In regulated software it is often the highest intent page on the site, because it is where a compliance buyer goes to find the reason to say no. Treating it as an afterthought means losing people at exactly the point they were closest to convinced.

So it got the same treatment as the homepage: what objection each answer is really addressing, what order those objections arrive in, and where the path forward sits once one is resolved.

The tracking schema is the part that lasts

I specified a sitewide GTM event schema for them: what gets measured, at what threshold, with what parameters, and named consistently enough that somebody who joins in a year can read it. Design recommendations get superseded. A measurement layer that is properly named and documented keeps paying out long after the redesign is forgotten.

Why I lead with instrumentation on inherited accounts

When I pick up an account where the tracking is missing or wrong, I audit and rebuild it, then collect sixty to ninety days of clean data before I write a single hypothesis. It feels slow to a client who wants recommendations in week two. It is the only way the recommendations end up being about their users rather than about my assumptions.
