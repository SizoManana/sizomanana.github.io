Decision trace

Why the reasoning lives in the file

Every board carries written notes beside the thing they affect. If a decision is deliberate, the file should say why. Otherwise the next person can easily read it as an accident and undo it.

That is also part of handoff. A note explaining why a panel expands in place rather than opening an overlay is more useful to the person inheriting the work than a polished screen with no rationale.

Template architecture

A wealth advice firm, ten page templates from one system

A national financial advice firm in the United States needed a site that supported several arrival modes: life situation, planning need, location and named adviser. The work is deliberately low fidelity of brand and high fidelity of structure.

ScopeHomepage plus parent and child templates for four page families
Widths1440 desktop and 375 mobile, considered separately
ModelShared skeletons that allow new audience and location pages without redesigning the system
FidelityPlaceholder copy, no client brand and no identifying information

What is deliberately not shown

The client is anonymised. Programme names, business metrics, partner references and identifying text were removed from the published artefacts. The portfolio only needs enough evidence to show the architecture and reasoning.

  • Orientation sits directly after the hero, before visitors face a long audience taxonomy. The current stage is marked as location, while later stages remain quieter links.

    orientation-strip rationale

  • A named scroll bridge follows the first card row because a clean row boundary can falsely signal that the section is finished. The bridge says what exists below rather than merely pointing downward.

    premature-exit risk

  • Category headings are written as user questions rather than internal labels so the visitor is not forced to classify themselves before exploring.

    taxonomy-to-language decision

  • Trust evidence repeats on pages that can receive organic entry traffic directly. A deep page cannot assume the homepage has already established credibility.

    entry-point assumption

Edge cases

The locations page was really a zero-result problem

A locations page looks like a directory problem until somebody searches a region with no nearby office. If the experience ends there, the site turns a motivated visitor into a dead end.

  • The office panel is inline rather than an overlay so it can be linked, shared, reached with the browser back button and stacked sensibly on a narrow screen.

    interaction and accessibility constraint

  • The no-office-nearby response contains one action. Somebody who has just been told distance is not a barrier needs a conversation, not another directory.

    zero-result recovery

  • The mobile map interaction was flagged as unresolved rather than quietly guessed. The likely model is region selector, office list, then office detail as the same three steps stacked.

    open question documented for handoff
